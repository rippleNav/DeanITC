Your Answer: It is hard to convince people because you are not just trying to move an opinion. You are trying to move a story they have built around themselves. They bring their history, their pride and their own clever defence of it. Sweet talk can catch them for a second. Confidence can impress them. But none of that changes the deeper ground. The words say the ground shifts like sand. You can pile on facts and they get buried, but the truth still shows somewhere. That means the problem is not always that people cannot see. Often they can see, but they are protecting the shape of their own story. The hopeful part is “a statue leaves” and “rest reaches, a sympathy.” Even a fixed position can move. It moves when there is rest instead of force, and sympathy instead of a fight. You convince more people by making room for them than by pressing harder. The door tends to open from the inside.
